Plan at a glance

Easy enough to follow, structured enough to work.

Tuesday: Lower A

Saturday: Lower B plus accessories

Alternating periodization

W1 EasyW2 HeavyW3 EasyW4 HeavyW5 EasyW6 HeavyW7 EasyW8 Heavy

Heavy means productive and controlled, never maximal. Easy weeks reduce joint and systemic fatigue while preserving the movement habit.

Easy-week prescription

  • Main movements: 2 × 10–15
  • Isolation: 2 × 12–20
  • Keep 3–4 repetitions in reserve
  • Use about 10–15% less load
  • Rest 60–90 seconds
  • Target 50–65 minutes

Heavy-week prescription

  • Main movements: 3 × 6–10
  • Secondary: 3 × 8–12
  • Isolation: 2–3 × 10–15
  • Keep 1–2 repetitions in reserve
  • Rest 2–3 minutes for main lifts
  • Target 65–80 minutes

Execution guide

The rules that make the program safe and repeatable.

Warm-up

  1. 5 minutes easy treadmill, bike or elliptical.
  2. Upper day: 10 controlled arm circles each way.
  3. Lower day: 10 box squats or very light leg extensions.
  4. First exercise: light × 12–15, moderate × 8, then on heavy weeks 70–75% × 3–5.

Warm-up sets do not count and must not create fatigue.

Double progression

Keep the same load until every working set reaches the top of its range with clean form and the assigned RIR. Then increase:

  • Upper compound machines: 2.5–5%
  • Lower machines: 5–10%, machine dependent
  • Isolation: smallest available increment

Never test a one-repetition maximum during this block.

RIR made simple

  • 4 RIR: approximately 4 clean reps remained
  • 2 RIR: approximately 2 remained
  • 1 RIR: approximately 1 remained
  • 0 RIR: muscular failure

Avoid 0 RIR on presses, rows, pulldowns and leg exercises. It may be used occasionally on the last arm-isolation set in a heavy week.

Pain and readiness

Muscular effort and burning are normal. Sharp, radiating or worsening joint pain is not. Stop and replace the movement.

Remove one set from every exercise if two nights of poor sleep, persistent soreness, unusual depletion, or two consecutive performance declines occur.

Cardio, nutrition & recovery

Enough structure to support the cut without obsessive tracking.

Walking and cardio

  • Average at least 7,000–8,000 steps daily.
  • Progress toward 8,000–10,000 if recovery stays good.
  • Two moderate sessions of 20–30 minutes weekly.
  • Suggested: Wednesday and Sunday.
  • Use a pace where short sentences remain possible.

Protein and meals

  • Daily protein target: 150–175 g.
  • Distribute across 3 meals plus an optional snack or shake.
  • Place useful carbohydrates 1–3 hours before and after training.
  • Measure oils, nuts, cheese and spreads because portions escalate quickly.

Simple plate: half vegetables, one quarter to one third lean protein, one quarter carbohydrates, moderate added fat.

Progress target

  • Aim for approximately 0.4–0.7 kg weekly loss.
  • Judge progress by weekly average weight, waist, photos and gym performance.
  • If stalled for 2 full weeks, change only one variable.
  • If losing over 0.8–0.9 kg weekly with weaker training or poor recovery, slightly increase food around workouts.

Five non-negotiables

Easy weeks remain easy.
Heavy means controlled, not maximal.
Progress only at the top of the rep range.
Technique wins over an extra repetition.
Do not add random exercises.

Progress check-in

Log once weekly under comparable morning conditions.

Safety note

This program is general fitness guidance, not medical treatment. Stop training and obtain qualified medical assessment for chest pain, dizziness, unusual shortness of breath, sharp pain, neurological symptoms, or symptoms that persist or worsen.
